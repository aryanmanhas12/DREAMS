MSc Public Health

The single most recognised public-health masters in the world for people who want to work in global health. Its distance-learning route is the cheapest credible way for an Indian doctor to hold an LSHTM degree without leaving the country.

Provider
London School of Hygiene & Tropical Medicine
Where
UK, London
Cost and funding
≈ £30,000 tuition · Commonwealth Shared & Chevening both fund it
Length
1 year full-time (or 2–5 yrs by distance learning)
When to apply
Opens Oct · rolling until courses fill · apply by Jan for funding

Who it is open to

  • MBBS or equivalent second-class honours
  • IELTS 7.0 (6.5 per component)
  • No work experience required for MSc Public Health

How to approach it

  1. Pick your stream first — Public Health, Health Services Management, Environment & Health, or Health Promotion. They have different cores.
  2. Register on the LSHTM online application portal and start early: the personal statement asks for a specific public-health problem you want to work on.
  3. Two academic references. For an MBBS student, one should be a department head who has seen you do research, not just teach you.
  4. Apply for Commonwealth Shared Scholarship in the SAME cycle. It is applied for through LSHTM, not separately, and closes months before term.
  5. If cost is the blocker, apply to the distance-learning MSc instead: roughly a third of the price, same degree certificate.

MPH-45 (accelerated) — Global Health, Epidemiology, or Health Policy

This is the single most important fact on this page for an Indian medical graduate: Harvard's 45-credit MPH requires a prior DOCTORAL degree, and MBBS counts. You are eligible for the accelerated route that most applicants are not, and it costs a year less than the 65-credit programme.

Provider
Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health
Where
USA, Boston
Cost and funding
≈ $75,000 total · Fulbright-Nehru and Inlaks both fund Harvard
Length
11 months (on-campus) or 2 years part-time online
When to apply
Opens Sept · Round 1 early Dec, final deadline Jan

Who it is open to

  • MBBS satisfies the doctoral-degree requirement
  • TOEFL/IELTS unless prior degree taught in English
  • GRE not required for most departments

How to approach it

  1. Confirm your MBBS is being read as the doctoral-equivalent qualification — state it plainly in the application, do not assume the reader knows what MBBS is.
  2. Apply through SOPHAS (the shared public-health application system), not directly to Harvard.
  3. Round 1 in early December carries the best funding odds. The January round is effectively for people who missed it.
  4. Ask for the WES or ECE credential evaluation early — Indian transcripts take weeks to verify.
  5. Apply to Fulbright-Nehru in parallel, one cycle ahead: it needs three years of work experience, so plan for it during internship and junior residency rather than immediately.

PhD in Neuroscience / Epidemiology / Computational Biology

The most important thing Indian medical students get wrong: US PhDs are PAID. You do not need a masters first, you do not need to self-fund, and MBBS is accepted as the prior degree. A funded PhD is a job with a salary, not a fee you have to raise.

Provider
US research universities (Stanford, MIT, UCSF, Michigan, UNC, Washington)
Where
USA, Various
Cost and funding
Fully funded: tuition waived + $38,000–$52,000 annual stipend + health cover
Length
5–6 years
When to apply
Opens Sept · deadlines 1–15 Dec almost universally

Who it is open to

  • MBBS accepted as the qualifying degree
  • Research experience is the single deciding factor — publications help but a real project matters more
  • TOEFL 100; GRE now optional at most programmes
  • Three strong letters, at least two from researchers
  • The money comes from the department, not from a fellowship you win first — which matters, because the NSF GRFP that dominates the search results is closed to you

How to approach it

  1. Build the research record FIRST. One completed project with an output beats five certificates of attendance.
  2. Email 3–5 potential supervisors in September with a specific, technical question about their work — ideally after reproducing one of their figures. This single habit converts applications more than any credential.
  3. Almost every US PhD deadline is 1–15 December. Work backwards: letters requested by early November, statement drafted by October.
  4. Apply to 8–12 programmes across a range of selectivity. Admission is noisy, and fit matters more than rank.
  5. Ignore the NSF Graduate Research Fellowship. It is the first thing you will find and it requires US citizenship, US national status or a green card, so an Indian applicant is excluded before the first question. Departmental funding is the actual route and it is attached to the admission offer itself.
  6. Never pay for a US PhD. If a programme offers admission without funding, that is a signal, not an opportunity.

OIST Research Internship

A fully funded four-to-six-month placement in a real laboratory, open to undergraduates, at an English-language graduate university with world-class neuroscience and computational biology. Two intakes a year rather than one, so missing a deadline costs six months instead of twelve. The catch that filters most Indian applicants is not academic: it is that you need written permission from your medical college to be away for four months, and that has to be negotiated long before the deadline.

Provider
Okinawa Institute of Science and Technology
Where
Japan, Onna, Okinawa
Cost and funding
Stipend, flights and accommodation covered · ¥5,000 application fee, non-refundable
Length
4–6 months (minimum 120 days)
When to apply
Two cycles a year — closes 15 October and 15 April

Who it is open to

  • Enrolled in the final two years of an undergraduate degree — for MBBS this means 2nd or 3rd professional year
  • Formal written permission from your medical college. This is the real gate; start it early
  • English proficiency; TOEFL and IELTS are optional, not required
  • Statement of purpose of no more than 250 words — the limit is strict

  1. Go to the faculty and project availability page first and pick two or three units that genuinely match you — computational neuroscience, molecular genetics, human evolutionary genomics. A named unit is what makes the application competitive.

  4. The 250-word statement is the hard part precisely because it is short. Say what you want to accomplish and how it fits where you are going. Nothing else fits.
  5. Pay the ¥5,000 fee and submit. Deadlines: 15 October for placements the following April–September, 15 April for October–March.
  6. Confirm current fees, cycle dates and eligibility on the OIST admissions page — internship terms are revised between cycles.

NZREX Clinical — practising medicine in New Zealand

The parallel route to Australia's AMC, with a smaller applicant pool and a country that is substantially cheaper to sit the process in. Worth knowing before you commit to the USMLE by default.

Provider
Medical Council of New Zealand
Where
New Zealand, Various
Cost and funding
House officer ≈ NZ$80,000–95,000/year once registered
Length
Qualifying exam → NZREX Clinical → PGY1 post → registration
When to apply
NZREX sittings run a few times a year and fill early

Who it is open to

  • MBBS from a school listed in the World Directory of Medical Schools
  • A qualifying exam FIRST — USMLE Step 1 and 2, AMC Part 1, PLAB Part 1 or MCCQE Part 1. NZREX is not an entry point on its own
  • Primary source verification of your degree through ECFMG's EPIC service
  • English evidence, and the ability to satisfy the Council you can communicate in a clinical setting

How to approach it

  1. Understand the gate before you spend anything: NZREX sits AFTER another country's exam. If you were going to sit PLAB or the AMC MCQ anyway, New Zealand costs you one extra exam rather than a whole new pathway.
  2. Start EPIC verification early. It is slow, it is administrative, and it blocks everything behind it.
  3. The part nobody mentions until late: passing NZREX does not register you. You then have to secure a PGY1 post with a training provider, and that post is the actual bottleneck. Ask about PGY1 availability before you sit the exam, not after.
  4. If Australia is also on your list, sequence them. AMC Part 1 satisfies the NZREX qualifying-exam requirement, so one exam can open both doors.
