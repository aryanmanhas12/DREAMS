Conferences that take student work

Attending is not a credential; presenting is. Each of these takes abstracts from students, and several fund the trip.

National society conferences that run student and PG paper sessions

Every major Indian specialty society runs an annual meeting with a paper session judged separately for students and postgraduates, and those sessions are chronically under-subscribed relative to the number of eligible students. This is where a network gets built. The person who chairs your session is often the person who later supervises your thesis or signs your reference. It is also the cheapest place in the world to learn how to answer a question you did not expect.

Provider
ANCIPS · IAPSMCON · IANCON · ASICON · APICON and their state chapters
Where
India, A different host city each year
Cost and funding
Student registration typically ₹2,000–6,000 · many societies waive it for presenting authors and several state chapters fund travel for award-paper finalists
Length
2–4 days
When to apply
Abstract deadlines usually fall 3–5 months before the meeting; most national meetings sit between November and March

Who it is open to

  • Membership of the relevant society, or a proposing member. Most have a low student rate
  • An accepted abstract, usually 250–300 words, submitted by the earlier abstract deadline rather than the registration deadline
  • A supervising consultant as co-author for most clinical work
  • Institutional ethics clearance for anything involving patient data — obtain it before you collect, not before you submit

How to approach it

  1. Pick the society that matches where you are actually heading. ANCIPS for psychiatry, IAPSMCON for community medicine and public health, IANCON for neurology, ASICON for surgery, APICON for internal medicine.
  2. Find the abstract deadline, not the conference date. It is usually months earlier and it is the only date that matters to you.
  3. Join your state chapter first. State meetings are smaller, cheaper, closer to home, and accept a much higher proportion of student abstracts.
  4. Ask explicitly about travel grants and registration waivers for presenting students. They exist at most societies and are almost never advertised.
  5. Go to the poster hall and talk to people whose posters you actually found interesting. That, not the sessions, is where the collaborations start.
  6. Verify dates, venue and abstract rules on the society's own site each cycle — these move every year.

Lindau Nobel Laureate Meeting

About six hundred young scientists spend a week with Nobel laureates, and 2027 is a medicine year, the first since 2023. From India the only door is the Department of Science & Technology, which pays the whole trip. That door is narrower than it looks and wider for doctors than people assume: in 2023 DST chose 16 of 223 applicants, and several were MD residents from AIIMS and government medical colleges.

Provider
Council for the Lindau Nobel Laureate Meetings, Germany
Where
Germany, Lindau
Cost and funding
DST pays international travel, local transport, twin-share lodging and meals · free to apply
Length
1 week: 27 June to 2 July 2027
When to apply
The 2027 meeting is Physiology/Medicine. Lindau's own form closes in November 2026; DST's Indian nomination call ended on 31 October in 2022 and 30 September in 2024, and the 2027 call was not yet posted at last check

Who it is open to

  • Indian citizen studying or working at an institution in India. DST's award excludes Indians studying or working abroad, and Lindau's open application is closed to anyone in a country with a partner, which India is
  • DST nominates master's students (MD and MS residents were selected in 2023), PhD students at least two years in, and postdocs under 35, ranked in the top three of their class. MBBS students are not among its categories
  • Two detailed reference letters in English, and no previous Lindau meeting

How to approach it

  1. Watch dst.gov.in's calls page from September. The application goes to DST first, forwarded by your head of institution, and only DST's shortlist is sent on to Lindau.
  2. Get the rank certificate from your head of department now. DST asks for it and it takes longer to obtain than anything else in the file.
  3. In past calls DST required a posted hard copy by the deadline as well as an emailed PDF. Read the new call for the exact format; a late or incomplete file is rejected outright.
  4. If you are an MBBS student, DST's categories do not include you. Keep a research record now so that a later medicine meeting finds you ready as a resident.

Neuroscience conferences with trainee travel awards

You do not attend these by paying. You attend them by submitting an abstract and applying for the travel award in the same breath. IBRO in particular exists to fund neuroscientists from countries like India, and its schemes are chronically under-applied.

Provider
SfN (USA) · FENS (Europe) · IBRO · ECNP · Indian Academy of Neurosciences
Where
Global, Rotating
Cost and funding
Travel awards of $1,000–2,500; IBRO explicitly funds researchers from lower-income countries
Length
4–6 days
When to apply
Abstract deadlines usually 5–7 months before the meeting

Who it is open to

  • An accepted abstract, usually
  • Trainee status
  • Travel award applications are separate and have earlier deadlines

How to approach it

  1. Submit the abstract AND the travel award application together. The award deadline is almost always earlier than you expect.
  2. IBRO runs travel grants, exchange fellowships and schools specifically for researchers in low- and middle-income countries. Start there.
  3. A poster at a national meeting first makes an international abstract far more likely to be accepted.

Global health & public health congresses with student bursaries

The big infectious-disease meetings set money aside for people from low- and middle-income countries, and they say so in their rules. The Union's full scholarships for presenters are reserved for low- and lower-middle-income countries, which includes India. The IAS gives priority to abstract presenters, applicants from LMICs, people under 35 and first-time recipients. Very few Indian medical students apply for either.

Provider
International AIDS Society · The Union (World Conference on Lung Health) · CUGH
Where
Global, Rotating
Cost and funding
In-person scholarships can cover travel, accommodation, a daily allowance and registration; sponsored registrations cover the fee only
Length
3–5 days
When to apply
Scholarship deadlines fall 4–8 months before each meeting, usually alongside or just after the abstract deadline

Who it is open to

  • An accepted abstract is the strongest route; the Union's full scholarship is for presenters and symposium speakers
  • India qualifies for the Union's full scholarships as a lower-middle-income country
  • IAS: aged 18 or over, and studying, working or volunteering in HIV

How to approach it

  1. Write the abstract first. At both meetings, presenting is what moves you into the fully funded group.
  2. Apply for the scholarship through your conference profile as soon as the abstract is in. The IAS closed its AIDS 2026 round in January for a July meeting, and nearly everything was awarded before the late-breaker stage.
  3. If the full scholarship does not come, ask for a sponsored registration. It removes the fee, which is often the largest cost for a meeting held in India or nearby.

Indian research conferences worth presenting at

Start here, not at an international meeting. A presented abstract at AIIMS INSIGHT costs a train ticket and gives you the thing every international application asks for: evidence you have presented work in public.

Provider
AIIMS INSIGHT · MEDICON · IAN · IPS · NIMHANS meetings
Where
India, Various
Cost and funding
₹500 – ₹5,000 registration; many offer student rates and some waive them
Length
2–3 days
When to apply
Abstract deadlines 2–4 months ahead

Who it is open to

  • An abstract
  • Student registration
  • Nothing else

How to approach it

  1. Present at a national meeting before applying to an international one. Acceptance rates and confidence both improve.
  2. Workshops attached to these conferences — multi-omics, molecular biology, AI in medicine — are certified and count.
  3. Volunteering to organise a session is worth more on an application than attending three.

OHBM, ASHG and ESHG — imaging and genetics meetings with trainee awards

These are where psychiatric imaging and genetics are actually argued out. OHBM has an active and welcoming trainee community, runs an educational course alongside the main meeting, and its abstract acceptance rate is high, which makes it a realistic first international presentation.

Provider
Organization for Human Brain Mapping · American & European Societies of Human Genetics
Where
Global, Rotating
Cost and funding
Trainee travel awards and heavily reduced registration; some meetings offer free virtual attendance
Length
4–5 days
When to apply
Abstract deadlines usually 4–6 months before the meeting

Who it is open to

  • An abstract
  • Trainee membership, which is cheap
  • Travel award applications are separate and earlier

How to approach it

  1. OHBM's educational day is worth attending on its own and is included in registration.
  2. Virtual attendance is often dramatically cheaper and sometimes free — check before assuming you cannot go.
  3. Submit the abstract first; the travel award follows the abstract in almost every case.

IJMS World Conference of Medical Student Research

A conference built specifically for medical students to present research to other medical students, run by a journal that exists for the same reason. Because it is online, it removes the two things that stop an Indian undergraduate presenting internationally — the airfare and the visa — and leaves only the abstract. For a first international presentation, this is the lowest-risk rung on the ladder, and the acceptance letter is real.

Provider
International Journal of Medical Students
Where
Global, Online and hybrid
Cost and funding
Low or no registration fee, and no flight. The online format is the point
Length
2 days
When to apply
Abstracts open months ahead; the 2026 meeting ran 11–12 July

Who it is open to

  • Current medical student, any year
  • An abstract — a case series, an audit or an original study all qualify
  • No institutional nomination needed

How to approach it

  1. Submit to their journal in the same cycle if the work is finished. The conference and the journal share an audience and a standard, and a presentation plus a publication from one project is the efficient version of this.
  2. Present the thing you actually did, including what went wrong. Student audiences ask better questions than senior ones because they are not being polite.
  3. Use this as the rehearsal before a physical international conference where a travel grant is at stake. The format is the same and the cost of a bad first outing is nothing.

Official page for IJMS World Conference of Medical Student Research — always the authority on dates and eligibility.

India Science Festival and the IISF science-communication events

The two events where Indian science talks to the public rather than to itself, and where a doctor who can explain things is unusually valuable. If your anger is about misinformation, or you have ever tried to explain a diagnosis to a frightened family, science communication is a real career track with funding attached, and these are the two places in India where the people already doing it are all in one room. Free, so the only cost is the train.

Provider
India Science Festival (IISER Pune) · IISF, Government of India
Where
India, Pune, and IISF moves city each year
Cost and funding
India Science Festival is free and open to all · IISF registration is nominal
Length
2 days
When to apply
India Science Festival runs in January at IISER Pune · IISF has run in October most years since 2015

Who it is open to

  • None to attend. Speaking and exhibiting slots are applied for months ahead
  • For IISF, participation often runs through institutional or Vijnana Bharati channels

How to approach it

  1. Go once as an attendee before trying to speak. Both festivals are unusually easy to attend and the format is not what most medical students expect.
  2. If you want a slot, watch the call in the autumn for the January festival — speaking applications close long before the event.
  3. Pair this with the science-writing work in the frontier list. A talk and a published piece are the two artefacts this field actually reads.

Official page for India Science Festival and the IISF science-communication events — always the authority on dates and eligibility.

Digital Health Scientific Symposium at the Global Digital Health Summit

A digital-health research prize a medical student can realistically win without a lab. The symposium takes 300-word abstracts on primary or secondary data, policy analyses and evidence-based editorials, and the 2025 first prize went to a narrative review of mental health apps. Its scientific committee includes a professor of community medicine from AIIMS Bhopal and a former Additional Deputy Director General of Health Services, so a paper here is read by people who shape Indian digital health.

Provider
Global Digital Health Summit, with the Academy of Digital Health Sciences
Where
India, New Delhi (2026: Bharat Mandapam)
Cost and funding
Prizes of ₹50,000, ₹30,000 and ₹20,000 for the top three papers. Summit passes are priced for industry: the 2026 ticket page starts at ₹5,600 for a basic delegate pass and lists no student rate
Length
Two days; the 2026 summit runs 20–21 November
When to apply
Abstract submissions for 2026 have closed. The call is posted on the summit's symposium page ahead of each November meeting

Who it is open to

  • A 300-word abstract, emailed to the organisers
  • Original research on primary or secondary data, a policy analysis, or an editorial that follows a research-backed approach
  • Topics across AI in clinical care, generative AI, public health, mental health, diagnostics and responsible AI

How to approach it

  1. Start a narrative or systematic review now on one digital-health question you care about. It is the format that won in 2025 and it needs no patients or ethics approval.
  2. Check the symposium page through 2027 for the next call. It closes some weeks before the November meeting, so a finished draft by late summer is the safe plan.
  3. Before paying for a pass, write to the organisers and ask whether accepted presenters attend free or at a reduced rate. The ticket page does not say.

One Young World health scholarships

Fully funded routes for young people already doing health work, covering flights and a hotel rather than just the ticket. It is the closest thing in this index to a shortcut into a global health network, and unlike most of what is here it rewards what you have already built rather than what you have studied. It is also crowded: One Young World reports 74,847 applications for 554 scholarships in 2024.

Provider
One Young World and its health partners
Where
Global, Summit city changes each year
Cost and funding
Partner health scholarships pay the summit place, economy flights, hotel and meals. The flagship Leading Scholarship decides travel and accommodation case by case
Length
A four-day summit (Cape Town, 3–6 Nov 2026), then ambassador membership
When to apply
Health partner streams run in spring (Sandoz 2026: 23 March to 27 April). The Leading Scholarship for the 2026 summit is open until 31 October 2026

Who it is open to

  • Sandoz health scholarship: aged 18 to 30. Leading Scholarship: aged 18 to 35
  • Demonstrable impact on a health problem: a project you actually ran, not a project you plan
  • Each partner stream has its own theme; the 2026 health stream was access to medicines and care
  • Applications are individual and free

How to approach it

  1. Start the project before you start the application. Every one of these streams asks what you have already changed, and that is the part that cannot be written the night before a deadline.
  2. Read the individual scholarship pages rather than the index. The streams differ sharply in theme and in who funds them, and applying to the wrong one wastes the cycle.
  3. Scale is not the test. A well-measured intervention in one district reads better than a vague national ambition, and the measurement is what most applications lack.
  4. If you miss the year, the work still counts. These calls repeat annually and a project with twelve more months behind it is a materially stronger application.

Official page for One Young World health scholarships — always the authority on dates and eligibility.

IARC Summer School in cancer epidemiology and prevention

WHO's cancer research agency trains epidemiologists, statisticians, physicians and oncologists in the methods behind cancer registries, screening and prevention, and it funds people from institutions in low- and middle-income countries to come. For an Indian MD in community medicine, pathology, radiotherapy or oncology, it is a funded week at the institution that publishes the carcinogen classifications.

Provider
International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC), the WHO cancer agency
Where
France, Lyon, after an online phase
Cost and funding
Scholarships for applicants working at institutions in low- and middle-income countries; each call sets what they cover
Length
An online phase, then one week in person at IARC in Lyon
When to apply
Runs every two years. The 2025 call opened 6 November 2024 and closed 6 January 2025; the 2027 call had not been posted at last check

Who it is open to

  • Aimed at working epidemiologists, statisticians, physicians, oncologists and public health specialists
  • Scholarships go to applicants working at institutions in low- and middle-income countries, which includes India
  • Two modules were offered in 2025: Introduction to Cancer Epidemiology, and Implementing Cancer Prevention and Early Detection

How to approach it

  1. Check IARC's learning site from October 2026. If the pattern holds, the call opens in November and closes in early January.
  2. Pick the module that matches your work: epidemiology if you are near a registry or a cohort, prevention and early detection if you run or evaluate screening.
  3. Ask your head of department now for the release letter. A week in Lyon in term time is agreed far more easily in advance than at selection.

Official page for IARC Summer School in cancer epidemiology and prevention — always the authority on dates and eligibility.

WHO Global Youth Competition: a 90-second video on influenza and COVID-19

One of the few WHO opportunities a first-year medical student can win outright, with no degree, fee or travel. The 2026 brief asked how you shield your circle from respiratory infections, and wanted videos that tackle myths directly. The prize is small in money and large in signal: a WHO certificate with your name on it and a slot on a global webinar, the kind of line that makes a later WHO internship application read as continuous rather than cold.

Provider
World Health Organization
Where
Global, Online
Cost and funding
Free to enter. The winners present their video at a global WHO webinar and receive a WHO certificate naming them
Length
One video of up to 90 seconds; winners announced in October
When to apply
The 2026 round ran 3 August to 13 September 2026 (23:45 CEST) and has closed; WHO has not announced a next round

Who it is open to

  • Students or young professionals aged 18 to 35
  • A video of no more than 90 seconds answering at least one of WHO's prompts; up to three entries, alone or as a team
  • Anyone who appears on screen must be over 18 and have consented; any use of AI must be declared

How to approach it

  1. Keep a short film ready rather than waiting for the call. The 2026 window was six weeks long, which is not much time to write, shoot and edit.
  2. Answer the prompt literally. WHO's reviewers score against a rubric that includes impact and how easily the video could be used in a community.
  3. Watch WHO's youth engagement pages and its Health for All Film Festival, which has run a separate Student Film Prize in past editions.
