Azim Premji Health Equity Fellowship: a paid year in a rural hospital

A year of medicine where it is hardest to practise, paid, mentored and with a CMC Vellore qualification attached. The MBBS track posts you to one of 19 hospitals the foundation trusts, among them Jan Swasthya Sahyog in Chhattisgarh, SEARCH in Gadchiroli, SEWA Rural in Gujarat and ASHWINI's Gudalur Adivasi Hospital in the Nilgiris, with a named mentor and a learning plan. Fellows also take CMC Vellore's certificate course in Foundations in Family Medicine and can go on to its Postgraduate Diploma in Family Medicine. For a doctor deciding what to do between NEET-PG attempts, it is a paid year of supervised work instead of a year of waiting.

Provider
Azim Premji Foundation, with 19 not-for-profit hospitals (MBBS track) and 15 (postgraduate track)
Where
India, Rural and tribal hospitals across India
Cost and funding
MBBS track: ₹40,000 a month plus ₹1.2 lakh on finishing the full 12 months. Postgraduate track: ₹60,000 a month plus ₹2.4 lakh on completion, with insurance and provident fund. Relocation and travel between sites are paid on both. The foundation calls it an honorarium that covers expenses, not a salary
Length
MBBS track: 12 months, mostly at one anchor hospital with a short visit to a second. Postgraduate track: January to December 2027 for the current batch
When to apply
Both 2026 calls are closed: the MBBS call on 15 March 2026 (selection February to March, results 10 April, start late May) and the postgraduate call for 2027 on 30 September 2026. The foundation keeps a register-your-interest form for the next round

Who it is open to

  • MBBS track: an MBBS graduate who has finished internship and any service bond, ideally with state registration, from the batches that graduated between 2020 and 2025
  • Postgraduate track: MD, MS or DNB in anaesthesia, community medicine, family medicine, general medicine, general surgery, obstetrics and gynaecology, or paediatrics
  • Hindi and English, plus the regional language of the site you are posted to

How to approach it

  1. Fill in the foundation's register-your-interest form now, so the next call reaches you instead of the other way round.
  2. Read the partner hospitals' own pages and pick two whose work you actually want to learn. Interviews go better when you can say why Gudalur and not Gadchiroli.
  3. If you are still in MBBS, spend a vacation week at one of these hospitals first. SEWA Rural, for one, asks Indian volunteers simply to email it.
  4. Plan around service bonds: the MBBS track wants yours finished before you join.

ICMR Short Term Studentship (STS)

The default first research project for every Indian medical student, and the one most people apply to a year too late. You can hold it ONCE, and only in 1st or 2nd year MBBS. Third and final year are ineligible, so the window genuinely closes.

Provider
Indian Council of Medical Research
Where
India, Your own college
Cost and funding
₹60,000 stipend · free to apply · no travel needed
Length
2 months
When to apply
Opens in spring · advertised close 30 May, extended to 10 June (5 pm) in the 2026 cycle

Who it is open to

  • 1st or 2nd professional MBBS only
  • A faculty guide from your own institution
  • Institutional Ethics Committee clearance
  • Free to apply

How to approach it

  1. Find your guide in the term BEFORE you need them. A professor who already knows your face says yes; an emailed stranger does not.
  2. Write the proposal around a question you can actually answer in two months with the patients your hospital already sees.
  3. Start IEC clearance immediately — ethics committees are the slowest part of the chain and they gate publication whether or not ICMR funds you.
  4. Check the date on the DHR portal every year rather than trusting any secondhand figure, including this one. This cycle moves constantly: it used to close around 10 January, the 2026 round advertised 30 May, and DHR then extended it to 10 June at 5 pm.
  5. Expect an extension and never plan for one. DHR extended almost every 2026-27 call by one to two weeks — STS and MD Thesis to 10 June, the main HRD research call to 15 June, the Young Medical Faculty PhD to 14 July. The extension is announced days before the original date, always as a PDF notice on the portal and nowhere else, so the only way to benefit is to be already watching. Aim for the advertised date; treat the extra fortnight as a reprieve if your ethics clearance slips, not as your plan.
  6. Even if you are not selected, finish the study. The stipend is the smallest part of the value; the publication is the whole point.

Official page for ICMR Short Term Studentship (STS) — always the authority on dates and eligibility.

IAS-INSA-NASI Summer Research Fellowship Programme

You pick actual scientists from a published list and they pick you back. Train fare is reimbursed, so a student with no money can spend a summer in a real laboratory at IISc, TIFR or NCBS at effectively zero personal cost. MBBS students are eligible and rarely apply.

Provider
Indian Academy of Sciences (joint with INSA and NASI)
Where
India, Host institute anywhere in India
Cost and funding
Stipend + second-class return train fare · free to apply
Length
2 months, usually May–July
When to apply
Applications open ~November · closed 31 January in the 2026 cycle

Who it is open to

  • Enrolled in MBBS or a science degree
  • Good academic record
  • Free to apply
  • You name up to 7 preferred guides from the directory

How to approach it

  1. Browse the guide directory BEFORE writing anything. Choose people whose recent papers you have actually read.
  2. The application asks for your preferred guides in order. Ranking a realistic spread beats listing seven Nobel-adjacent names.
  3. Apply in the November–December window for a summer placement.
  4. Train fare reimbursement means the real cost to you is close to zero. Say yes to a placement far from home.

Khorana Program for Scholars

A fully funded American summer research placement that a current MBBS student can hold — no graduation required, no fees, travel paid. The academic bar is set lower for MBBS candidates, 65 per cent rather than 80. Whether it runs again for 2027 is not yet known, so treat it as one to watch rather than to plan around.

DBT Govt of India · IUSSTF · WINStep Forward
Where
USA, US host university

Length
10–12 weeks, summer
When to apply
The last published call closed 7 October 2024, for summer 2025. No later call had been posted at last check; past deadlines fell in the first week of October

Who it is open to

  • Currently enrolled MBBS at a recognised Indian institution
  • PRE-FINAL year — at least one year of your course must remain
  • 65 % and above for MBBS applicants (80 % for other streams)

  1. Confirm your year status counts as pre-final BEFORE you invest in the application. Email WINStep Forward and ask directly. It takes ten minutes and saves a cycle.
  2. Apply through the IUSSTF visitation-programmes portal.
  3. IUSSTF and WINStep match you to up to three US professors. You do not have to find the lab yourself, which removes the hardest barrier.
  4. The statement of purpose should name a specific research area, not a general interest in the United States.

Amgen Scholars Programme (Asia)

Fully funded summer research at Kyoto, Tokyo, the National University of Singapore, Tsinghua and, since it joined, IIIT Hyderabad. The Asia programme has no citizenship rule and takes undergraduates enrolled at universities in Asia, which is the cleanest eligibility line in this index for an Indian student, and the Hyderabad site makes one version of it a domestic flight. The other regions are where people waste applications: Europe takes only students enrolled in a Bologna-process country, Australia only students in Oceania, and the US only its own students.

Provider
Amgen Foundation; Amgen India funds the IIIT Hyderabad site
Where
Asia, IIIT Hyderabad, Kyoto, Tokyo, NUS Singapore or Tsinghua
Cost and funding
Housing, travel support and a stipend, with details that vary by host; the Asia symposium is paid for · free to apply
Length
About 8–10 weeks, summer
When to apply
Opens 1 November; all five Asia hosts close 1 February 2027. Programme dates are announced later

Who it is open to

  • Enrolled undergraduate at a university in Asia, India included, with at least the first year complete by the time the programme starts
  • You must not graduate before the programme, and must return for at least one more semester of undergraduate study after it, so plan it for a summer before your final year
  • English: TOEFL iBT 72, IELTS 5.5, Cambridge FCE or TOEIC 1095 if English is not your first language
  • A strong academic record and a stated interest in a PhD
  • The Europe, Australia and US programmes are closed to students enrolled in India

How to approach it

  1. Apply only through the Asia programme. Each host runs its own application, so read each host's page from 1 November.
  2. You may apply to more than one host. Do so, and put IIIT Hyderabad on the list: far fewer Indian medical students know it hosts than know about Kyoto.
  3. Sort the English score early. For most Indian students English is not the first language, and a test booked in January can miss a 1 February deadline.
  4. Write the statement about a PhD you actually want. That interest is in the eligibility rules, not a courtesy line.

Official page for Amgen Scholars Programme (Asia) — always the authority on dates and eligibility.

Lindau Nobel Laureate Meeting

About six hundred young scientists spend a week with Nobel laureates, and 2027 is a medicine year, the first since 2023. From India the only door is the Department of Science & Technology, which pays the whole trip. That door is narrower than it looks and wider for doctors than people assume: in 2023 DST chose 16 of 223 applicants, and several were MD residents from AIIMS and government medical colleges.

Provider
Council for the Lindau Nobel Laureate Meetings, Germany
Where
Germany, Lindau
Cost and funding
DST pays international travel, local transport, twin-share lodging and meals · free to apply
Length
1 week: 27 June to 2 July 2027
When to apply
The 2027 meeting is Physiology/Medicine. Lindau's own form closes in November 2026; DST's Indian nomination call ended on 31 October in 2022 and 30 September in 2024, and the 2027 call was not yet posted at last check

Who it is open to

  • Indian citizen studying or working at an institution in India. DST's award excludes Indians studying or working abroad, and Lindau's open application is closed to anyone in a country with a partner, which India is
  • DST nominates master's students (MD and MS residents were selected in 2023), PhD students at least two years in, and postdocs under 35, ranked in the top three of their class. MBBS students are not among its categories
  • Two detailed reference letters in English, and no previous Lindau meeting

How to approach it

  1. Watch dst.gov.in's calls page from September. The application goes to DST first, forwarded by your head of institution, and only DST's shortlist is sent on to Lindau.
  2. Get the rank certificate from your head of department now. DST asks for it and it takes longer to obtain than anything else in the file.
  3. In past calls DST required a posted hard copy by the deadline as well as an emailed PDF. Read the new call for the exact format; a late or incomplete file is rejected outright.
  4. If you are an MBBS student, DST's categories do not include you. Keep a research record now so that a later medicine meeting finds you ready as a resident.

Official page for Lindau Nobel Laureate Meeting — always the authority on dates and eligibility.

The free skill stack: Python, statistics, epidemiology, bioinformatics

Every credential on this page assumes skills you can acquire for nothing. Coursera's financial aid application takes fifteen minutes and is approved for Indian students at high rates, including for Johns Hopkins epidemiology and biostatistics certificates. EMBL-EBI's entire bioinformatics catalogue is free and unregistered.

Provider
Coursera financial aid · edX audit · EMBL-EBI · OpenWHO · Kaggle · Software Carpentry
Where
Online, Remote
Cost and funding
Genuinely ₹0 — Coursera grants financial aid to Indian students on request for almost every course
Length
Self-paced
When to apply
Always open

Who it is open to

  • An internet connection
  • Nothing else

How to approach it

  1. Apply for Coursera financial aid rather than paying. Write two honest paragraphs; approval takes about 15 days.
  2. The order that works: Python basics → pandas → statistics → the specific method your field uses.
  3. Free and genuinely good: Johns Hopkins Data Science and Epidemiology on Coursera, EMBL-EBI bioinformatics, OpenWHO for public health emergencies, Kaggle Learn for practical Python, Software Carpentry for reproducible workflows.
  4. Do CITI or NIH Good Clinical Practice training free online. It is a formal requirement for many research placements and takes one afternoon.
  5. Twenty minutes of code a day beats a weekend sprint every month. Nobody bridges medicine and computation heroically.

Research methods: systematic reviews, GCP, statistics

A first-year medical student can be the first author on a systematic review. It requires no laboratory, no funding, no ethics clearance and no permission — only method and persistence. This is the fastest legitimate route from zero publications to one.

Provider
Cochrane · PRISMA · NIH · CITI Program · Nature Masterclasses
Where
Online, Remote
Cost and funding
Mostly free; Cochrane offers free access to learners in many low- and middle-income countries
Length
Days to weeks
When to apply
Always open

Who it is open to

  • Access to PubMed
  • A specific, answerable question
  • A co-author or two

How to approach it

  1. Learn the method properly through Cochrane Interactive Learning — free for learners in many eligible countries.
  2. Register your protocol on PROSPERO before you start screening. This is what separates a real review from a literature summary.
  3. Follow PRISMA reporting standards exactly; reviewers check.
  4. Use Zotero for references and Rayyan for screening. Both free.
  5. Complete GCP training via CITI or the NIH — free, certificated, and required for many clinical research placements.

Google Summer of Code with INCF and neuroscience organisations

The most overlooked opportunity on this entire site for a medical student who codes. INCF and several neuroscience and bioinformatics organisations participate every year, you work remotely from India, you are paid, and you need no institutional affiliation, no professor and no visa. A merged contribution to a tool that real neuroscientists use is a credential a principal investigator can verify in thirty seconds.

Provider

Where
Online, Remote
Cost and funding
Stipend paid by Google — roughly $1,500–3,300 depending on project size and country
Length
12 weeks, summer
When to apply
Organisations announced Feb · student applications late Mar–Apr

Who it is open to

  • Aged 18 or over — no degree or enrolment requirement at all
  • Ability to write code and, more importantly, to communicate on a public issue tracker

  1. Watch for the participating-organisations list in February and look for INCF, NeuroStars, the Turing Way, Open Bioinformatics Foundation and similar.

  4. Even an unsuccessful attempt leaves you with merged contributions to a real scientific tool, which is worth more than most certificates.

Public datasets you can analyse from anywhere, tonight

The single most under-used fact in Indian medical research: you can run a study on half a million people from a laptop in Jammu. UK Biobank grants access to approved researchers worldwide. The Psychiatric Genomics Consortium publishes its summary statistics openly. India's own National Family Health Survey is fully public and chronically under-analysed. No ethics delay, no funding, no permission — only a question and the method to answer it.

Provider
UK Biobank · PGC · GenomeIndia / IBDC · OpenNeuro · MIMIC · NHANES · NFHS · DHS
Where
Online, Remote
Cost and funding
Free or a modest access fee; several require nothing but registration
Length
As long as you want
When to apply
Always open

Who it is open to

  • An answerable question. This is the actual bottleneck, not access
  • R or Python
  • Some datasets need a supervisor or institutional affiliation on the application; many need nothing

How to approach it

  1. Start with something with no gatekeeping: NFHS for Indian population health, OpenNeuro for brain imaging, PGC summary statistics for psychiatric genetics, MIMIC for critical care (after a short free training module).
  2. Reproduce a published figure before attempting anything original. It teaches the pipeline and gives you something to show.
  3. Register the analysis plan before you look at the outcome. It is what separates a study from a fishing expedition.
  4. Publish the code publicly. A repository someone else can run is a stronger credential than a certificate, and it is entirely within your control.

BIRAC Biotechnology Ignition Grant (BIG)

The largest sum in this index that an individual student can hold, and almost no medical student applies. BIRAC's own eligibility names medical degree holders alongside PhDs and biomedical engineers, so a clinician with a diagnostic or device idea is inside the intended audience rather than at its edge. Doctors see the unmet need every day in outpatients; the people who usually win these grants have to go looking for it.

Provider
BIRAC, Department of Biotechnology
Where
India, Anywhere in India
Cost and funding
Up to ₹50 lakh grant-in-aid over 18 months · no equity taken
Length
Up to 18 months
When to apply
BIRAC's page says calls open on 1 January and 1 July for about 45 days, but the 25th call actually ran 1–30 November 2025 and none has been listed since (checked September 2026). Watch the call-for-proposals page rather than the calendar

Who it is open to

  • An idea at nascent or planning stage. A prototype is not required to apply
  • Medical degree holders are named in the scheme's own eligibility, alongside students and academics
  • A No Objection Certificate from your institution if you are still enrolled
  • Funds for an individual applicant go into a dedicated auditable no-lien account

How to approach it

  1. Start from a problem you have watched go unsolved on a ward round, not from a technology you find interesting. The clinical observation is the part nobody else on the applicant list has.
  2. Read the BIG user guide before writing anything. It states plainly what stage of idea the scheme funds, and most rejected applications are simply at the wrong stage.
  3. Get the institutional NOC moving early. It is administrative rather than difficult, and it is the step most likely to miss the date.
  4. Attach yourself to a BIRAC-recognised incubator. They exist to shepherd exactly this application and their support materially changes the odds.
  5. If the idea is a device rather than a molecule, run this alongside the School of International Biodesign fellowship in this index rather than choosing between them.

Official page for BIRAC Biotechnology Ignition Grant (BIG) — always the authority on dates and eligibility.

International Neuroscience Doctoral Programme

One of the best-funded neuroscience institutes in Europe, built deliberately as an international programme, teaching in English, on the Lisbon waterfront. The structure is the thing worth noticing: a year of rotations before you choose a laboratory, which means you are not gambling five years on a supervisor you met once over a video call. A medical degree is a real advantage in this pool rather than an oddity.

Provider
Champalimaud Foundation, Lisbon
Where
Portugal, Lisbon
Cost and funding
Full stipend, no tuition, plus a first year of rotations before you commit to a laboratory
Length
4–5 years
When to apply
Applications typically open ~November and close ~February

Who it is open to

  • A degree in medicine, biology, engineering, physics, psychology or mathematics. The programme deliberately recruits across all of them
  • No neuroscience background required; they train it
  • English; no Portuguese needed
  • Strong quantitative ability matters more than laboratory experience

How to approach it

  • Researcher stream: an MPH or MSc in implementation research, plus either a PhD in public health or an MD with at least three years of research experience and publications, and at least one peer-reviewed article on an IR theme
  • Practitioner stream: an MPH or equivalent and three years of programme management at a health ministry, NGO or similar body
  • Employed at a university, research institute, health ministry or public health institution, with a letter confirming study leave

How to approach it

  1. Check the degree rules first. With an MBBS alone you do not qualify for either stream; this is the fellowship to aim for after an MPH.
  2. Bring a real problem. The strongest applications name one specific failure in one specific place, such as a stockout, a follow-up rate or a referral that never happens, rather than a topic.
  3. Get your employer's study-leave letter early. Six months away needs protected time, and departments agree to it far more slowly than you expect.
  4. The host university changes between rounds, so confirm where the next cohort sits and whether the dates hold when the new call appears.

ICGEB PhD: the New Delhi programme and the Arturo Falaschi Fellowships

An intergovernmental research centre with one of its three laboratories in New Delhi. There are two doors and they admit different people. The ICGEB-JNU PhD for Indian nationals names MBBS in its eligibility list outright, runs entirely in New Delhi, and is paid through the national fellowship you already hold. The international Falaschi fellowships add Trieste and Cape Town with their own stipend, but name a BSc (Honours) or an MSc rather than MBBS. Almost no medical students hear of either, because ICGEB recruits through biotechnology departments and never through medical colleges.

Provider
ICGEB — International Centre for Genetic Engineering and Biotechnology
Where
India, New Delhi, Trieste (Italy) or Cape Town (South Africa)
Cost and funding
New Delhi PhD: paid by the JRF you bring · Falaschi PhD: €1,500/month in Trieste, US$1,122 in New Delhi, ZAR 16,500 in Cape Town, plus tuition, travel and health insurance · free to apply
Length
3 years PhD with a possible fourth · 1–12 months (short-term) · postdoctoral fellowships separately
When to apply
New Delhi PhD: yearly call, the 2026 one closed 30 May with interviews 22–24 June. Falaschi: the next round opens January 2027 and closes 31 March 2027; a September round (expression of interest by mid-August) covers short-term and postdoctoral places only

Who it is open to

  • New Delhi PhD (Indian nationals): MBBS is named, alongside a first-class MSc, M.Tech, M.V.Sc, M.Pharm or a four-year research BSc (Honours)
  • It also needs a fellowship already in hand: CSIR, UGC, ICMR, DBT, DST-INSPIRE, BINC or an equivalent JRF, valid for at least four years. ICMR-JRF is the usual one for a medical graduate.
  • Falaschi PhD: nationality of an ICGEB Member State (India is one); a BSc (Honours) for Trieste and an MSc for New Delhi or Cape Town. MBBS is not named, so ask the fellowships office before building an application on it.
  • Falaschi: a written statement of support from the ICGEB group leader you want to join. It is mandatory.
  • Short-term places need current enrolment in a PhD elsewhere; postdoctoral places need the PhD. No age limit on any of them.

How to approach it

  1. Sit ICMR-JRF first. The New Delhi PhD takes MBBS graduates only with a valid JRF-level fellowship, and that fellowship is what pays you.
  2. Watch ICGEB's fellowship page in spring for the ICGEB-JNU announcement. In 2026 it ran two streams, Human Health & Industrial Biotechnology and Plant Science, and closed on 30 May.
  3. Before the interview, read the New Delhi group pages and pick two or three groups whose published work you can discuss in detail.
  4. For Trieste or Cape Town, the Falaschi round opens in January and closes 31 March. Write to the fellowships office to ask whether your MBBS meets the degree rule, and allow two months for the group leader's statement, which is the step that usually fails.
  5. Once you are enrolled in a PhD in any member state, the September round (expression of interest by mid-August, full application by 30 September) funds short stays in an ICGEB lab.

DHR Short-Term and Long-Term Fellowship Abroad

The Indian government will pay for you to train abroad and come back, and almost no medical graduate knows this scheme exists. It is designed explicitly to build a pool of health research personnel by putting Indian clinicians inside international laboratories in emerging areas of clinical and health science. The age limits are the giveaway that this is not aimed at students — below 45 for the long-term fellowship and below 57 for the short-term — which means it stays open for essentially your entire working career, unlike almost everything else on this site.

Provider
Department of Health Research, Ministry of Health & Family Welfare
Where
India, Held at an international host institute
Cost and funding
Government-funded fellowship abroad under the DHR Human Resource Development scheme · free to apply
Length
15 days to 3 months (short-term) · 6 to 12 months (long-term)
When to apply
Annual call, opened 22 April with a 30 June close in the 2026-27 cycle, then extended

Who it is open to

  • Long-term (6–12 months): below 45 years on the closing date
  • Short-term (15 days–3 months): below 57 years on the closing date
  • A health research role and an international host institution willing to take you
  • Free to apply, through the DHR/ICMR international fellowship portal

How to approach it

  1. Secure the host first. The scheme funds you to go somewhere specific; it does not find the somewhere. Approach the group the way you would for any fellowship — read their work, write to the group leader, ask for a defined period.
  2. Read the eligibility and guidelines on the DHR HRD page before drafting, because the terms are revised between cycles and the amended guidelines are published as a separate PDF.
  3. Choose short-term if you are still building a case. Fifteen days to three months is enough to learn a technique and start a collaboration, and it is far easier to be released from your post for.
  4. Note the selection warning DHR prints itself: applying to a specific international institute carries no guarantee of selection. Have a second host in mind.
  5. This call has been extended in recent cycles, as most DHR calls are. Aim at the advertised date and treat any extension as a margin, not a plan.

School of International Biodesign — iFellowship in Medical Device Innovation

A government-funded year that pays a medical graduate a real stipend to invent a medical device, inside a team with engineers, designers and MBAs, hosted between AIIMS and IIT Delhi. The Stanford biodesign method treats the clinician as the origin of the invention. You are in the room because you are the only person who has watched the problem happen. For anyone who has ever improvised a fix on a ward and thought someone should build this properly, this is the structured version of that thought, and it is one of very few routes on this site that pays you to leave clinical work temporarily without leaving medicine.

Provider
AIIMS New Delhi & IIT Delhi, under the Department of Biotechnology
Where
India, New Delhi
Cost and funding
₹60,000/month stipend, plus travel costs and health cover · free to apply
Length
1 year
When to apply
Applications accepted twice a year

Who it is open to

  • Graduates and postgraduates from medicine, engineering, design or management — MBBS qualifies directly
  • Selection favours a demonstrated track record of innovation and willingness to work in a mixed team
  • No engineering background needed; being the clinician is the contribution

How to approach it

  1. Before applying, keep a needs notebook on the wards for a month — every workaround, every improvised fix. The application and the interview both reward specific observed problems over general enthusiasm for innovation.
  2. Apply as someone who will still be a doctor afterwards. The strongest medical fellows treat this as clinical training in a different medium, not as an exit.
  3. Two intakes a year means missing one costs six months rather than a year. Check the current cycle on the fellowship page.
  4. Look also at the SIB-SHInE programme run by IIT Kanpur with KGMU Lucknow if Delhi does not suit. The model has been replicated.

ICMR-NIE research methods courses: Health Research Fundamentals, and the BCBR for postgraduates

Free research methods teaching from ICMR's own epidemiology institute. The Basic Course in Biomedical Research is the one the National Medical Commission requires of every postgraduate admitted since 2019 and of medical teachers. ICMR-NIE's own FAQ says MBBS students cannot enrol in it and directs them to its sister course, Health Research Fundamentals, which is open to undergraduates. Doing that one during MBBS means your first project and your ICMR-STS proposal start from a sound design.

Provider
ICMR National Institute of Epidemiology, Chennai, on NPTEL/SWAYAM
Where
Online, Self-paced
Cost and funding
Free to learn. The Basic Course in Biomedical Research charges ₹1,000 for its proctored certificate exam, halved for SC and ST candidates and people with a disability above 40%
Length
Self-paced video lectures; the BCBR has 23 lectures, each with an assignment
When to apply
Enrol at any time. BCBR exams run several times a year and need 50% or more in every lecture assignment first

Who it is open to

  • MBBS students and anyone else interested in health research: Health Research Fundamentals
  • BCBR: medical postgraduates (MD, MS, MCh, DM) admitted from 2019-20 onwards, and teachers in medical institutions

How to approach it

  1. If you are in MBBS, enrol in Health Research Fundamentals on NPTEL/SWAYAM and finish one module a week.
  2. Use what it teaches on sample size and study design before you write an ICMR-STS proposal, not after.
  3. Once you are a postgraduate, take the BCBR early. The NMC requires it, and its notifications on the ICMR-NIE page say when it must be complete.
