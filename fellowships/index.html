Fellowships open to Indian medical graduates

Fellowships are where a medical career stops being only clinical. Several of these take people still in training, and the entries say which.

DBT/Wellcome Trust India Alliance Fellowships

The single most important structural fact for an Indian clinician-researcher: India Alliance runs a Clinical and Public Health Research fellowship stream where a medical degree plus a research record can substitute for a PhD. You can lead your own funded research programme in India without ever doing a doctorate. Almost nobody uses this.

Provider
India Alliance
Where
India, Various
Cost and funding
Early Career Fellowship: full salary + research consumables + travel, for 5 years
Length
5 years
When to apply
Two calls a year, typically Jan and Jul

Who it is open to

  • MBBS/MD/MS/DNB with a demonstrated research record. A PhD is NOT required for the clinical stream
  • A host institution in India that will support you
  • A mentor and a clear five-year research plan

How to approach it

  1. Read the Clinical and Public Health Research Fellowship criteria specifically. It is a different track from the basic-science one.
  2. You need a host institution and a sponsor before you apply. Build that relationship during residency, not after.
  3. Two calls a year means a missed deadline costs six months, not a year.
  4. This is the endgame for anyone who wants to stay in India and still run real research. Work backwards from it.

Official page for DBT/Wellcome Trust India Alliance Fellowships — always the authority on dates and eligibility.

Wellcome Early-Career and Career Development Awards

Wellcome funds researchers to build a group where they already are, rather than requiring permanent relocation. For a doctor who wants a research career without emigrating, this is the model, but read the next line before you spend a month on it.

Provider
Wellcome Trust, UK
Where
UK, Various / home country
Cost and funding
Salary + research costs, held at an eligible host organisation
Length
5 years (Early-Career), 8 years (Career Development)
When to apply
Distinct calls with fixed closing dates; check the scheme page each quarter

Who it is open to

  • Health-related research with a specific, answerable question
  • An eligible host organisation and a sponsor
  • A doctorate OR equivalent clinical research experience

How to approach it

  1. If you are based in India, start at India Alliance, not here. Wellcome's own guidance routes India-based applicants there, and the International Training Fellowship that older guides point you to is closed. The page for it now exists only to say so.
  2. Wellcome's India route is the DBT/Wellcome India Alliance, which is separately listed on this site and whose clinical stream does not require a PhD. That is the entry worth your month.
  3. Come back to Wellcome direct once you hold a position at an eligible host organisation outside India, or a joint appointment that gives you one.
  4. Wellcome cares intensely about the research question. Vague proposals about 'AI in healthcare' do not survive triage; a specific, answerable question does.

Official page for Wellcome Early-Career and Career Development Awards — always the authority on dates and eligibility.

Prime Minister's Research Fellowship (PMRF)

The best-paid doctoral fellowship in India by a wide margin — more than most junior residents earn, with a research grant attached and a travel allowance for international conferences. It is aimed at technology and science disciplines, which means a medical graduate moving into computational biology, health data science or biomedical engineering is an unusual and often welcome applicant rather than a competitor in a crowded field.

Provider
Ministry of Education, Government of India
Where
India, IITs, IISc, IISERs, NITs, central universities
Cost and funding
₹70,000–80,000/month rising to ₹80,000 in later years, plus a ₹2 lakh annual research grant
Length
4–5 years
When to apply
Two cycles a year, typically closing around March and October

Who it is open to

  • Admission to, or application for, a PhD at a participating institution — IITs, IISc, IISERs, NITs and select central universities
  • The direct-entry channel assesses your academic record and research proposal
  • Interdisciplinary applicants are explicitly encouraged, which is where a medical degree becomes an advantage rather than an oddity

How to approach it

  1. Identify a PhD programme at a participating institution first — biomedical engineering, computational biology and health informatics groups at the IITs and IISc all take medical graduates.
  2. Apply through the PMRF portal in one of the two annual cycles; the direct-entry channel is the relevant one for most applicants.
  3. Your research proposal carries the application. Write it about a specific answerable question, not a field you find interesting.
  4. Contact the prospective supervisor before applying. At Indian institutions this matters as much as it does abroad and is done far less often.

Official page for Prime Minister's Research Fellowship (PMRF) — always the authority on dates and eligibility.

DBT-JRF (Biotechnology Eligibility Test) & ICMR-JRF

These are the examinations that turn an Indian PhD from something you pay for into something that pays you. A DBT or ICMR fellowship is portable. You take it to whichever institution accepts you, which inverts the usual power dynamic when approaching a supervisor. Very few medical students sit these because nobody tells them they are eligible.

Provider
Department of Biotechnology / Indian Council of Medical Research
Where
India, Any recognised institution
Cost and funding
₹37,000–42,000/month plus HRA and an annual contingency grant
Length
5 years of doctoral support
When to apply
DBT-BET around Feb–Apr · ICMR-JRF around Mar–Jul

Who it is open to

  • MBBS holders are eligible for ICMR-JRF; DBT-BET is open to postgraduates in life sciences and to medical graduates
  • Written examination followed by an interview
  • The fellowship is portable across institutions

How to approach it

  1. Sit ICMR-JRF if your interest is biomedical and clinical; DBT-BET if it is molecular and biotechnological.
  2. Because the fellowship travels with you, approach a supervisor holding one and the conversation changes completely. You are bringing funding, not asking for it.
  3. CSIR-UGC NET JRF is a third route, though eligibility for MBBS holders varies by cycle — check the current notification rather than relying on what a senior told you.

Official page for DBT-JRF (Biotechnology Eligibility Test) & ICMR-JRF — always the authority on dates and eligibility.

INSPIRE Fellowship & Faculty Scheme

The INSPIRE Faculty scheme is the interesting one and almost nobody outside basic science knows it exists: it gives an early-career researcher an independent position and a grant at an Indian institution, without needing to wait for a conventional faculty vacancy. For someone determined to build a research career in India rather than emigrate, it is one of the few genuine ladders that exists.

Provider
Department of Science and Technology, Government of India
Where
India, Any recognised institution
Cost and funding
Doctoral fellowship ₹37,000–42,000/month · INSPIRE Faculty gives an independent position with a research grant
Length
5 years doctoral · 5 years faculty
When to apply
Doctoral calls usually mid-year; faculty calls announced periodically

Who it is open to

  • Doctoral scheme: strong academic record at the qualifying level
  • Faculty scheme: a PhD, usually within a few years of completion
  • Host institution needed for the faculty scheme

How to approach it

  1. For the faculty scheme, secure the host institution's agreement before applying. That is the practical gate.
  2. Check the current eligibility notification for medical graduates; DST criteria have shifted between cycles.
  3. This pairs naturally with an India Alliance fellowship as the next step up.

Faculty for the Future Fellowship

For women from developing countries in STEM, and India qualifies. Fifty thousand US dollars a year, renewable until you finish, for a doctorate anywhere in the world. It is one of the largest awards an Indian woman in science can hold and the applicant pool is a fraction of what the sum warrants — largely because it is filed under 'engineering' in most people's minds and never reaches medical students at all.

Provider
Schlumberger Foundation
Where
Any, Any host institution abroad
Cost and funding
Up to USD 50,000 a year for a PhD, renewable to completion; postdoctoral awards up to USD 40,000
Length
Full doctorate or postdoc
When to apply
Opens ~September · closes ~November

Who it is open to

  • Female, and a citizen of a developing or emerging economy — India qualifies
  • Dual citizenship with a developed country makes you ineligible
  • PhD or postdoctoral level only — masters programmes are not funded
  • Applied to, admitted by, or enrolled at a host institution abroad
  • A record of community outreach and of encouraging girls into science is assessed, not decorative
  • Commitment to return to your home country afterwards

How to approach it

  1. Confirm the current URL and cycle at the Schlumberger Foundation site. The programme has been renamed alongside the company and links move.
  2. The outreach requirement is real and weighted. If you have taught, mentored or run anything for girls in science, document it with numbers before you apply.
  3. You do not need a confirmed place to start the application, only to have applied, so this runs in parallel with your doctoral applications, not after them.
  4. Applications open around September and close in November for the following academic year.

Official page for Faculty for the Future Fellowship — always the authority on dates and eligibility.

L'Oréal-UNESCO For Women in Science & AAUW International Fellowships

A whole tier of funding exists specifically because women leave science at every career stage, and it is chronically under-applied in India relative to the number of eligible candidates. AAUW funds women who are not US citizens to study in the United States, which most Indian applicants never discover because they search for 'scholarships for Indians' rather than 'fellowships for women'.

Provider
L'Oréal Foundation with UNESCO · American Association of University Women
Where
Any, Various
Cost and funding
L'Oréal India For Young Women in Science: ₹2.5 lakh · International Rising Talents: €15,000 · AAUW International: USD 20,000–50,000
Length
1 year, often renewable
When to apply
L'Oréal India around Oct–Dec · AAUW closes mid-November

Who it is open to

  • Female applicants
  • AAUW: non-US citizen, studying or researching in the US, closes mid-November
  • L'Oréal India runs a separate national programme with its own criteria
  • Research record and outreach both assessed

How to approach it

  1. Search by both axes. Almost everyone searches nationality and stops; the gendered schemes are a second, largely separate pool.
  2. AAUW's International Fellowship is the largest of these and specifically funds women who are not US citizens.
  3. Regional and national For Women in Science awards often have far smaller fields than the global one — check the India programme first.

Fogarty/NIH Global Health Fellowship — NPGH LEADERs Consortium

A full year of US-government-funded mentored research, done mostly in India with a paired US mentor and a short spell at their university. The University of Washington, Indiana, Michigan and Minnesota consortium all take Indian trainees. This is not a relocation programme; it is US-calibre funding and mentorship landing on Indian soil, which is the opposite of every other route in this section.

Provider
NIH Fogarty International Center, via the Northern Pacific Global Health consortium
Where
India, An Indian research institution, with 1–3 months at a US partner site
Cost and funding
A living stipend sized to local cost of living, plus research and travel support — paid whether you are based in India or briefly at the US site
Length
12 months
When to apply
Cycle typically opens in autumn and closes in winter for a start the following July

Who it is open to

  • Doctoral-level trainee in a health profession — MD, MBBS-with-research-registration, or enrolled in a relevant doctoral programme
  • Citizen or permanent resident of India
  • A proposed India-based mentor and, ideally, informal contact with a US-side mentor before applying
  • Available to spend 1–3 months at the US consortium university during the fellowship year

How to approach it

  1. Read the NPGH LEADERs consortium's current partner list on fogartyfellows.org. The US-side mentor you approach should already have India ties or an active collaborator here.
  2. Draft a one-year research plan with your India-based mentor before you touch the application; the US partnership is easier to arrange once the local half exists.
  3. Apply through the portal in the autumn cycle for a July start. This is a once-a-year window, not rolling.
  4. Budget the 1–3 month US stint separately: visa timing (a J-1 exchange visa, typically) needs its own runway alongside the fellowship paperwork.

Official page for Fogarty/NIH Global Health Fellowship — NPGH LEADERs Consortium — always the authority on dates and eligibility.

Rotary Peace Fellowship

The most under-applied full scholarship open to a doctor whose anger is about conflict, displacement or health in emergencies. Because it is framed as peacebuilding rather than health, medics rarely look at it, and yet the fellows who work on health in conflict zones are exactly who the programme wants. The certificate route at Chulalongkorn or Makerere is the unusual part: eleven weeks, fully funded, and designed for people already working, so it does not require you to abandon a job for two years.

Provider
The Rotary Foundation
Where
Global, Peace Centres in the USA, UK, Japan, Sweden, Uganda and Thailand
Cost and funding
Tuition and fees, room and board, return airfare, and all field-study and internship costs. Up to 50 masters fellowships a year.
Length
15–24 months (masters) · 11 weeks (professional development certificate)
When to apply
One annual round, closing 15 May; Peace Centre assignments announced the following November

Who it is open to

  • Proficiency in English, and a bachelor's degree — MBBS qualifies
  • Masters route: at least three years of relevant work or volunteer experience
  • Certificate route: at least five years, and it is aimed at people mid-career
  • A commitment to international understanding and peace, evidenced by what you have actually done

How to approach it

  1. Decide which of the two routes you are actually eligible for before writing anything. The masters and the certificate have different experience bars and different audiences.
  2. Selection by Rotary does not admit you to the university. You apply to the host institution separately, and that admission is not guaranteed. Budget for two applications, not one.
  3. You need a Rotary club endorsement. Find your district's contact early; this is the step that catches people out, and it cannot be done in the final week.
  4. Write the essays about a specific conflict or displacement problem you have seen in your own work. Health in emergencies, mental health after disaster, and access under blockade are all squarely within scope and are argued far less often than the generic peace essay.
  5. Selected fellows hear in November for a course starting the following year, so this is an eighteen-month lead time from application to arrival. Plan the gap deliberately.

Official page for Rotary Peace Fellowship — always the authority on dates and eligibility.

ICMR International Fellowship for Indian Biomedical Scientists

Twelve Young Scientist fellowships a year, at three thousand US dollars a month, to work in an overseas laboratory and come back. Twelve is a very small number, and that is the argument for applying, not against it, because the pool that knows this exists is smaller still. It sits alongside the DHR Fellowship Abroad rather than competing with it: this one is ICMR's own biomedical scheme with a stipend attached, and both are advertised on the same portal in the same season, so you can see them in one visit.

Provider
Indian Council of Medical Research
Where
India, Held at an overseas host laboratory
Cost and funding
US$3,000 a month for Young Scientists, covering accommodation and living · free to apply
Length
3–6 months (Young Scientist) · 10–15 days (Senior Scientist)
When to apply
Annual call through the DHR portal, alongside the other HRD schemes

Who it is open to

  • Indian biomedical scientist with a research position — MD/MS/DNB or PhD
  • A host laboratory abroad that has agreed to take you
  • Only twelve Young Scientist places and six Senior Scientist places a year
  • Free to apply, through the DHR e-portal

How to approach it

  1. Secure the overseas host before the call opens. As with every scheme of this shape, the invitation is the slow part and the form is the fast part.
  2. Check which tier you are applying under. Young Scientist buys you three to six months, which is long enough to learn a technique properly; the Senior route is ten to fifteen days and is for people going to do something specific.
  3. Watch the same DHR portal that carries STS and the Fellowship Abroad. The HRD calls cluster, and one visit in April or May shows you all of them.
  4. Write the proposal around a technique or dataset you cannot access in India. Reviewers are asking what the trip buys that staying would not.

Official page for ICMR International Fellowship for Indian Biomedical Scientists — always the authority on dates and eligibility.

INYAS — Indian National Young Academy of Sciences membership

India's only officially recognised academy for young scientists, and its eligibility explicitly names MD in Medical Sciences alongside a PhD, which almost no Indian clinician realises. It is the closest thing this country has to a peer body for early-career researchers, and medical members are scarce enough that a clinician-scientist joining changes the composition of the room rather than adding to it. Being inside it is how you stop being the only person in your institution working on your question.

Provider
Under the mentorship of the Indian National Science Academy (INSA)
Where
India, National, based wherever you work
Cost and funding
Membership rather than money. A five-year term, with funded meetings and outreach programmes attached
Length
5-year non-renewable term
When to apply
Annual membership call, most recently open through the first half of the year

Who it is open to

  • PhD in science or engineering, OR an MS/MD in medical sciences
  • Under 40 on 31 December of the call year — women may seek relaxation to 41 with justification
  • Indian citizen, living and working in India
  • Currently holding an academic position, temporary or permanent

How to approach it

  1. Check the age line first, because it is hard and it arrives sooner than people plan for. If you are finishing an MD at 32, you have roughly eight cycles, not a career's worth.
  2. The application is judged on independent contribution, not on volume. One line of work you clearly own reads better than a list you were the fourth author on.
  3. Hold an academic position at the time of applying. A purely clinical post without an institutional academic role does not satisfy it.
  4. Look at the current membership before writing. Medical members are few, which is the argument for applying rather than against it, and it tells you which gap you would be filling.

Official page for INYAS — Indian National Young Academy of Sciences membership — always the authority on dates and eligibility.

Global Health Policy Fellows Programme

A paid policy fellowship that names India in its own list of focus countries, alongside Ghana, Kenya, Myanmar, Nigeria and Sri Lanka. Most global health policy work is done to countries like yours by people from somewhere else; this is one of the few doors built the other way round, and it is aimed at health financing and governance rather than bench research.

Provider
Center for Policy Impact in Global Health, Duke University
Where
Global, Remote and Durham, USA
Cost and funding
Paid fellowship · stipend set per cohort
Length
Fixed term, set per cohort
When to apply
Calls are announced on the Center's own page rather than aggregated anywhere

Who it is open to

  • Applications invited from candidates in low- and middle-income countries
  • India is named among the preferred focus countries
  • A background in health policy, health financing and delivery, or government financing of health services
  • Experience conducting health research, quantitative or qualitative, is preferred rather than required
  • Early- to mid-career researchers particularly encouraged

How to approach it

  1. Read two of the Center's own working papers before applying and reference them specifically. The applicant pool here is small enough that a generic letter is obvious.
  2. If your background is purely clinical, build one piece of health-systems work first. A costing exercise, a district-level access audit, an analysis of an existing NSSO or NFHS dataset. The open-data entry in this index is where to start tonight.
  3. Health financing is the part clinicians consistently skip and the part this field actually runs on. A free course in health economics closes more of the gap than another clinical posting.
  4. Watch their page directly. This role is not advertised through the usual scholarship aggregators.

Official page for Global Health Policy Fellows Programme — always the authority on dates and eligibility.

DTH-Lab Fellowship — digital health governance

Digital health is being governed right now, by a small number of people writing the rules for data, AI and platforms in health systems. This fellowship exists specifically to put students and early-career researchers from low- and middle-income countries in that conversation instead of leaving them to inherit its conclusions.

Provider
Digital Transformations for Health Lab
Where
Global, Remote
Cost and funding
Supported fellowship · terms set per call
Length
Project-length, alongside study or work
When to apply
Applications have opened in the spring, with an April close in the last cycle

Who it is open to

  • Students or early-career researchers aged 30 or under
  • Based in a low- or middle-income country, which includes India
  • An interest in policy and governance rather than in building the technology itself

How to approach it

  1. The age ceiling is real and it is 30, which makes this one of the few things in this index that is genuinely easier to reach as a student than as a consultant. Do not save it for later.
  2. You do not need to code. The work is governance, and a clinician who can explain what a data rule does to a real patient is the rarer contributor.
  3. Read the Lab's published reports before writing. Their output is short, specific, and tells you exactly what kind of thinking they want.
  4. Pair this with the WHO digital health material and one open-data project, so the application shows a line of interest rather than a single application.

Official page for DTH-Lab Fellowship — digital health governance — always the authority on dates and eligibility.

Takemi Program in International Health

A year at Harvard to work on health systems, aimed squarely at people who will go back and run them. Worth knowing about early even though you cannot apply yet, because it changes what a mid-career plan looks like: the people who get this spent their twenties building a health-systems record, not only a clinical one.

Provider
Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health
Where
USA, Boston
Cost and funding
Funded research year · fellows secure or are awarded support per cohort
Length
One academic year
When to apply
Applications open in the autumn for the following academic year

Who it is open to

  • Mid-career health professionals and scholars. This is not an entry-level route
  • A track record in health systems, policy or health economics
  • A defined research project to pursue during the year

How to approach it

  1. File this under 'later' deliberately, then work backwards from it. The record it requires takes a decade to build and almost none of it is clinical.
  2. The Takemi fellows list is public. Read where five of them worked before their year, and you have an unusually honest map of what this career actually looks like.
  3. In the meantime, the routes that lead here are in this index already: an MPH, a health-systems research post, or the Duke policy fellowship above.

UNICEF Internship Programme

A paid UN internship open to every nationality, with health, nutrition and WASH among the posted fields, and it takes people still enrolled or within two years of graduating. Most students assume the UN is closed to them until they have a masters and a decade of work. This one is not, and it posts all year.

Provider
United Nations Children's Fund
Where
Global, Country offices, HQ, or remote
Cost and funding
Paid: a monthly stipend up to about US$1,700 depending on duty station · travel and visa support where funding allows · no application fee
Length
6 to 26 weeks
When to apply
No single deadline — posts appear continuously on the careers portal

Who it is open to

  • At least 18 and enrolled in an undergraduate, graduate or PhD programme, or graduated within the past two years
  • Fluency in one UNICEF working language: English, French or Spanish
  • Open to all nationalities, so Indian students and recent graduates are eligible
  • No application fee at any stage

How to approach it

  1. Treat the careers portal as a feed rather than a deadline. Posts appear all year, so check it fortnightly instead of waiting for a cycle that does not exist.
  2. Filter for health, nutrition and WASH, and read the country-office posts as seriously as the headquarters ones. A field office gives you more responsibility and less competition.
  3. The two-year post-graduation window is the part people miss. If you are in internship year, this is open to you now and closes quietly two years after you qualify.
  4. Remote posts exist. If you cannot leave India this year, that removes the usual objection entirely.

Official page for UNICEF Internship Programme — always the authority on dates and eligibility.

TDR Clinical Research Leadership Fellowship

A WHO programme built specifically so that clinical trials in diseases of poverty are led from the countries where those diseases actually are, rather than run remotely from Europe. It trains you to run a trial, not to assist on one, and it is explicitly reserved for people working in low- and middle-income countries, which for once puts an Indian applicant inside the target group rather than competing against it.

Provider
TDR — the WHO Special Programme for Research and Training in Tropical Diseases
Where
Global, Placed with a partner research institution or product developer
Cost and funding
Funded placement — TDR covers the fellowship; your home institution holds your post open
Length
12 months
When to apply
Calls run roughly annually; watch the capacity-strengthening page

Who it is open to

  • Employed in a clinical research role for at least 24 months at a registered institution in an LMIC
  • Demonstrable experience in trial-related work in diseases of poverty
  • Your institution must agree to release you and take you back

How to approach it

  1. The institutional commitment is the hard part, not the form. Secure your head of department's written agreement before you draft anything.
  2. The 24-month clinical research requirement is counted properly. Get your trial roles documented — protocol work, site coordination, data management all count, and most people undercount their own.
  3. Read TDR's own published trial priorities before writing. Applications that name a disease TDR is actually funding work on read entirely differently from generic ones.
  4. If you do not yet clear the 24-month bar, the Postgraduate Training Scheme and the Implementation Research fellowship on this list are the earlier rungs of the same ladder — do one of those first.

Official page for TDR Clinical Research Leadership Fellowship — always the authority on dates and eligibility.

TDR Implementation Research Leadership Fellowship

Implementation research is the discipline that asks why a treatment that works in a trial does not work in a district, which is the single most common frustration of anyone who has done a rural posting. This fellowship teaches it formally, hosts it in the global South rather than flying you to Geneva, and costs nothing. For a doctor whose anger is about the gap between guideline and reality, it is the most direct training that exists.

Provider
TDR (WHO), with UNICEF, UNDP and the World Bank
Where
Global, Hosted at a partner university in the global South
Cost and funding
Fully funded, including the taught component · free to apply
Length
About 12 months, blended
When to apply
Annual call, recent rounds closing end of January

Who it is open to

  • Working in a health-related role in a low- or middle-income country
  • A concrete implementation problem you can actually study where you work
  • Institutional support to take on the taught component alongside your post

How to approach it

  1. Bring a real problem. The strongest applications name one specific failure in one specific place — a stockout, a follow-up rate, a referral that never happens — rather than a topic.
  2. Get your supervisor's agreement in writing early; the blended format needs protected time and departments agree to it far more slowly than you expect.
  3. The host university changes between rounds, so check where the current cohort sits before assuming a location.
  4. Recent rounds have closed at the end of January, which means drafting over December. Confirm the current date on the TDR page. This call has moved before.

Official page for TDR Implementation Research Leadership Fellowship — always the authority on dates and eligibility.

Arturo Falaschi Fellowships (PhD, short-term and postdoctoral)

An intergovernmental research centre with one of its three laboratories in New Delhi, which makes this the rare world-class fellowship you can hold without leaving India, and the rare Indian position that comes with an automatic route to Trieste or Cape Town. India is a founding member state, so Indian nationals are inside the priority group rather than competing as foreign applicants. Almost no medical students have heard of it, because it recruits through biotechnology departments and never through medical colleges.

Provider
ICGEB — International Centre for Genetic Engineering and Biotechnology
Where
India, New Delhi, Trieste (Italy) or Cape Town (South Africa)
Cost and funding
Full stipend, health insurance and travel · free to apply
Length
1–12 months (short-term) · 3–4 years (full PhD) · 2 years (postdoctoral)
When to apply
Main call closes 31 March; a second short-term round runs to 30 September

Who it is open to

  • A degree in life sciences, medicine or a related field — MBBS is accepted
  • A written statement of support from the ICGEB group leader you want to work with. This is mandatory and there is no way around it.
  • For the short-term route, current enrolment in a PhD elsewhere; the long-term route is the PhD itself

How to approach it

  1. Choose the group before the fellowship. Read the ICGEB New Delhi group pages, find two or three whose actual published work you can discuss, and write to those group leaders directly.
  2. The supporting statement from the group leader is the application's spine. Allow two months to secure it. This is the step that fails, not the form.
  3. If you are still in MBBS, the short-term route is not yet open to you; build toward it by finishing a real project first, then apply as a PhD student or a graduate.
  4. Note the two windows. The main call closes at the end of March, and a separate short-term round runs through to the end of September, so a missed deadline is not a missed year.

DHR Short-Term and Long-Term Fellowship Abroad

The Indian government will pay for you to train abroad and come back, and almost no medical graduate knows this scheme exists. It is designed explicitly to build a pool of health research personnel by putting Indian clinicians inside international laboratories in emerging areas of clinical and health science. The age limits are the giveaway that this is not aimed at students — below 45 for the long-term fellowship and below 57 for the short-term — which means it stays open for essentially your entire working career, unlike almost everything else on this site.

Provider
Department of Health Research, Ministry of Health & Family Welfare
Where
India, Held at an international host institute
Cost and funding
Government-funded fellowship abroad under the DHR Human Resource Development scheme · free to apply
Length
15 days to 3 months (short-term) · 6 to 12 months (long-term)
When to apply
Annual call, opened 22 April with a 30 June close in the 2026-27 cycle, then extended

Who it is open to

  • Long-term (6–12 months): below 45 years on the closing date
  • Short-term (15 days–3 months): below 57 years on the closing date
  • A health research role and an international host institution willing to take you
  • Free to apply, through the DHR/ICMR international fellowship portal

How to approach it

  1. Secure the host first. The scheme funds you to go somewhere specific; it does not find the somewhere. Approach the group the way you would for any fellowship — read their work, write to the group leader, ask for a defined period.
  2. Read the eligibility and guidelines on the DHR HRD page before drafting, because the terms are revised between cycles and the amended guidelines are published as a separate PDF.
  3. Choose short-term if you are still building a case. Fifteen days to three months is enough to learn a technique and start a collaboration, and it is far easier to be released from your post for.
  4. Note the selection warning DHR prints itself: applying to a specific international institute carries no guarantee of selection. Have a second host in mind.
  5. This call has been extended in recent cycles, as most DHR calls are. Aim at the advertised date and treat any extension as a margin, not a plan.

School of International Biodesign — iFellowship in Medical Device Innovation

A government-funded year that pays a medical graduate a real stipend to invent a medical device, inside a team with engineers, designers and MBAs, hosted between AIIMS and IIT Delhi. The Stanford biodesign method treats the clinician as the origin of the invention. You are in the room because you are the only person who has watched the problem happen. For anyone who has ever improvised a fix on a ward and thought someone should build this properly, this is the structured version of that thought, and it is one of very few routes on this site that pays you to leave clinical work temporarily without leaving medicine.

Provider
AIIMS New Delhi & IIT Delhi, under the Department of Biotechnology
Where
India, New Delhi
Cost and funding
₹60,000/month stipend, plus travel costs and health cover · free to apply
Length
1 year
When to apply
Applications accepted twice a year

Who it is open to

  • Graduates and postgraduates from medicine, engineering, design or management — MBBS qualifies directly
  • Selection favours a demonstrated track record of innovation and willingness to work in a mixed team
  • No engineering background needed; being the clinician is the contribution

How to approach it

  1. Before applying, keep a needs notebook on the wards for a month — every workaround, every improvised fix. The application and the interview both reward specific observed problems over general enthusiasm for innovation.
  2. Apply as someone who will still be a doctor afterwards. The strongest medical fellows treat this as clinical training in a different medium, not as an exit.
  3. Two intakes a year means missing one costs six months rather than a year. Check the current cycle on the fellowship page.
  4. Look also at the SIB-SHInE programme run by IIT Kanpur with KGMU Lucknow if Delhi does not suit. The model has been replicated.
